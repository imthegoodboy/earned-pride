# Notes

The judge's brief, in order: genuine earned pride on the subject; a concrete scene with a real person; pride earned by felt effort, never stated; it lands by the last word; and freshness (no trophies, podiums or graduations).

Candidates considered:
1. A grandmother who left school at twelve signs her own name on a bank form and slides the pen back. This was the first draft and is moving, but late-life literacy is a familiar image.
2. A father who never learned to swim lets go of the pool wall at 63 and floats "like he owned it". **Chosen, revised.**
3. A father with a stutter reads his whole wedding toast without looking up, then folds the paper into his pocket. Strong, but a toast is a stock scene.
4. Three winters after a stroke, Mum buttons all five coat buttons while the bus waits. Strong, but the ending leans on the driver.
5. A seven-year-old carries a full watering can across the yard and pours it on the wrong plant with great care. Funny, but the pride is small.
6. A night janitor who failed the exam twice tapes his electrician's licence inside his locker door. Good, but it reads as a summary of the effort.
7. A kid rides the whole car park without looking back. Bike-riding is stock.
8. A grandmother parallel-parks the church van on the ninth try. Fun, but the difficulty is thin.

Why the swimmer: the effort is felt inside the line ("a whole summer of Tuesday lessons", "afraid of water all his life"). The act is concrete and seen happening (he lets go of the wall, arms wide). The pride arrives in the last words without being named ("like he owned it"), and the image is fresh. It is 201 characters, ASCII only.
